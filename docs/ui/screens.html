What each part is

MarkerPartDocDescription
1Week strip§4, §7, §8 Fifteen bars, one per week of the semester, each sized by the hours of outstanding work projected into that week and coloured by the load band it falls into. The dashed line marks the student's weekly capacity. The current week is outlined and its number is emphasised on the axis; the next week over capacity is marked in the warning colour. Above the chart, two figures state hours landing this week against hours available, and which week is the next wall.
2Wall card§4, §2 States the next week whose projected work exceeds capacity, with both figures given in the heading. Below it, a single recommended action: which assignment to begin, which week to begin it in, and the hours other students recorded for that assignment. The figures are produced by the computation layer, and the action is a link into planning that week.
3Coming up§4, §7 A two-column grid of the next assignments due. Each card carries its category badge and colour bar, its title, the week and course it belongs to, and a difficulty meter with the hours students typically recorded. Ordered by how soon the work starts rather than by due date alone.
4Course chips§4 A horizontally scrolling row, one chip per course, showing the course code and attendance remaining with a status dot. Courses without an attendance policy read "no policy", which is distinct from zero absences left. Each chip opens that course's detail.
5Week figures§5 Hours landing in the current week against hours available, and the count of outstanding items. The same comparison as screen 1, narrowed to the present week.
6Task rows (option A)§5, §6 Outstanding work grouped under Overdue, Due this week, and Next week. Each row carries a category tile, the title, the course, and a difficulty meter, with a circular checkbox on the right that marks the work done. Work that is not yet due but should be started is flagged "start now".
7Task cards (option B)§5, §6 The same groups and the same content arranged as square cards in two columns. The category tile and checkbox sit at the top of each card and the metadata at the bottom, so the title has the middle of the card to itself.

Assignment categories

Every assignment carries one category. The category sets the colour and the two-letter tile used wherever that assignment appears. Categories are assigned at intake; the set is fixed so that colour stays meaningful across screens (§7).

CategoryTileCovers
ExamEXMidterms, finals, and quizzes that require study
ProjectPJMulti-week builds, demos, and presentations
Problem setPSProblem sets and short graded exercises
ReadingRDReadings and reading responses
WritingWREssays, reports, and written deliverables
Lab / homeworkHWLabs and routine weekly homework

Difficulty

The three-bar meter and the hours beside it report the time students recorded for that assignment in previous semesters, not a judgement of how hard it is. One bar is light, two is moderate, three is heavy relative to the student's weekly capacity. Where no hours have been recorded, the meter is omitted rather than estimated (§2).
