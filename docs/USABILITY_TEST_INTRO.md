# Usability Test: Introduction and Background Questions

This is the opening portion of the Crunch Week usability test script. It is read or
paraphrased by the facilitator at the start of each session, before any task is given.
It covers the welcome, what the app is, what we are testing, how the session runs, the
think-aloud protocol, and the background questions.

The actual test tasks are written separately (see the tasks issue) and are **not**
described here. This section must not tell a participant how to do those tasks.

---

## 2. What the app is (purpose and vision)

> The app is called **Crunch Week**. It is a mobile app for college students.
>
> The idea behind it is simple. When you have a lot of classes, each one has its own
> assignments, readings, and exams, and it is hard to see the whole picture at once.
> Crunch Week takes the work from all of your classes and puts it on a single timeline
> for the semester, so you can see how heavy each week is. It highlights the weeks where
> the work piles up beyond the time you have available — we call those "crunch weeks" —
> and it suggests which assignment to start first and when to start it, so you can get
> ahead before those busy weeks arrive.
>
> As you use it, you will see a few screens: a **Forecast** that shows the workload
> across the whole semester, a **This week** screen that lists the work you should be
> doing now, and a **Courses** screen with details about each class. You do not need to
> memorize any of that — just explore it as you would normally.

---

## 3. What we are testing (and what we are not)

> I want to be clear about one thing: **we are testing the app, not you.** There are no
> right or wrong answers. You are not being graded on your performance, and you cannot break anything. If something is confusing or
> does not work the way you expect, that is exactly the kind of thing we want to find
> out, because it tells us what to fix. In fact, if you struggle with something, you are
> doing us a favor.
>
> You will not hurt our feelings by giving honest feedback. We built this app, and we
> fully expect it to have rough edges. Please feel free to say what you really think,
> including criticism.

---

## 4. How the session will work

> Here is what the next 30 to 45 minutes will look like.
>
> 1. **A few background questions** (a few minutes). I will ask how you normally organize
>    your schoolwork and whether you have used apps like this before.
> 2. **Trying out the app** (most of the time). I will give you a few tasks to try, one
>    at a time. I will not show you how to do them — I want to see how you figure them
>    out on your own, the way you would with a new app on your own phone.
> 3. **A few wrap-up questions** at the end about how it felt overall.
>
> A few housekeeping notes:
> - Please go at your own pace. There is no time limit on any task, and I would rather
>   you take your time than rush.
> - If you get stuck, tell me that you are stuck and keep thinking out loud. I may not
>   be able to help right away, because I want to see how you work it out, but I will
>   let you know if we need to move on.
> - With your permission, I would like to ______________ *(take notes / record the
>   screen / record audio)* so I do not miss anything. Is that okay with you?
>   - Participant consent: ______________ *(yes / no)*

## 5. The think-aloud protocol

> While you use the app, please keep talking. Say out loud whatever is going through
> your head as you look at the screen and decide what to do next. This is the most
> important part of the session, because your thinking is the thing we most want to
> understand.
>
> To give you an idea of what is useful, things like these are all helpful:
> - What you are looking for right now. *"I'm looking for where to add a class."*
> - What you expect to happen before you tap something. *"I think tapping this will show
>   my week, because it says 'This week'."*
> - What you notice on the screen. *"This bar is taller than the others, so I guess that
>   week is busier."*
> - When something is confusing or surprising. *"I wasn't expecting that to happen."*
> - What a label or number means to you, or whether you are unsure. *"I'm not sure what
>   this '15 hours' line is."*
> - What you would do next and why. *"I'd tap here because I want to mark this done."*
>
> There is no need to plan your sentences or make them sound nice — just narrate what you
> are thinking, even if it feels trivial or you are not sure it matters. If you go quiet
> for a while, I may gently say "what are you thinking right now?" to keep the commentary
> going. That is not a hint, just a reminder to keep talking.

## Notes for the facilitator (do not read aloud)

- Keep your own commentary minimal during the tasks. Do not demonstrate the app or tell
  the participant where to tap. If asked "what should I do here?", respond with a
  neutral prompt such as "what would you normally do?" rather than a solution.
- If the participant stops thinking aloud, a neutral "what are you thinking right now?"
  is enough. Avoid leading questions that suggest an answer.
- The app currently runs with sample semester data and no real sign-up, so the
  participant will start with a semester already loaded. If asked, you can say the
  screens are pre-filled with example courses for this test.
- Capture verbatim quotes where you can — they are more useful later than summaries.
